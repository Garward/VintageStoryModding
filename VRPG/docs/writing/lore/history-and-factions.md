# History & Factions

Reference for rarity provenance, NPC-flavored naming, and anything that needs a
"who made this and why" answer that isn't the Rust World.

## The Seraphim

The player character is a seraph: a tall, blue-skinned humanoid with the ability
to revive after death, indistinguishable from a human in speech and behavior but
physically unmistakable. Seraphim appeared in the world with no explanation and
no memory of where they came from. To the remnant human survivor settlements,
they are a known but uncommon sight, not a myth. Nobody, including the seraphim
themselves, knows why they are here.

This is the single best "unexplained but not mysterious-for-mysterious's-sake"
element in the base game. Use it for provenance flavor when an item's origin
should read as "found on or made by a seraph" without needing to explain more
than that. The mystery is already load-bearing in the base game and does not
need VRPG to resolve it.

## Classes as Backstory Fragments

The six playable classes (Commoner, Hunter, Malefactor, Clockmaker, Blackguard,
Tailor) are not just stat presets. Each is tied to lore books, scrolls, and
tapestries implying a much older, larger civilization than what survives on the
surface. See `classes.md` for their actual flavor text, which is the primary
tone reference for VRPG's own writing. A seventh, unused "unknown class" exists
in the game's files with its own dialogue, never officially released or
explained. Worth knowing about, not worth building on top of, since it isn't
canon.

## Traders

Nine trader types spawn in claimed wooden carts across the surface: Agriculture,
Artisan, Building Materials, Clothing, Commodities, Furniture, Luxuries,
Survival Goods, and Treasure Hunter. All deal exclusively in rusty gears (see
`economy.md`). Traders restock on a delivery timer and are the primary gateway
to the base game's story content. The Treasure Hunter trader specifically
begins it. Worlds set to Homo Sapiens mode have no traders and no story content
at all, which confirms traders are load-bearing for narrative delivery, not just
a shop.

Use trader types as the basis for any new NPC-facing vendor or quest-giver
naming: they already read as plain occupational categories (what they sell, not
who they are), which fits the "legible before flavorful" principle directly.

## Ruins & "Days of Old"

Structures generate across the surface and underground as ruins, sometimes
containing blocks, like aged wooden planks, that cannot be crafted by the
player, implying an earlier civilization the player never meets directly. This
is the source for "provenance" rarity flavor (see the style guide's before/after
table): an item found in a ruin reads as older and more storied than one bought
from a trader, without needing an invented timeline or named historical era.
Do not invent proper-noun eras ("the Sundering," "the First Age"). The base
game deliberately leaves this vague, and VRPG should too.
