# Cosmology & Elements

Reference for anyone naming or writing flavor text for VRPG's damage types,
status effects, or anything else that would be a "magic school" in a generic ARPG.
This is the canonical source; the style guide's element table is copied from here.

## The Rust World

Vintage Story already establishes that the world the player stands on has a
degraded twin: the Rust World. Temporal stability is a live measurement of how
close the player is to falling into it. Low-stability areas glitch, fog over,
warp their terrain, and eventually let the Rust World's effects apply directly to
the player. Temporal rifts are fixed points where it bleeds through constantly.
Temporal storms are the whole world's stability dropping at once, on no fixed
schedule the player can predict.

Drifters, bowtorns, shivers, bells, and locusts are not "monsters" in the generic
sense. They are what happens to matter, and once flesh, that spends enough time
soaked in Rust bleed. Drifters escalate through named tiers as the concentration
of Rust rises with depth: surface, deep, tainted, corrupt, nightmare. A
double-headed drifter only spawns during a temporal storm, when the bleed is
strongest everywhere at once.

This is the one myth VRPG's elemental system draws from. It does not need a
second one. Every "element" below is a named symptom of the same underlying
phenomenon, not a separate school of magic.

## Element Remap

| Generic term | VRPG name | Grounding | Legibility check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical | Physical | Ordinary force, no bleed involved | reads as "physical", unchanged |
| Fire | Fire | Rust-corroded metal and mineral turning reactive once destabilized enough to combust | still reads as "the burn one" |
| Cold | Cold | Stasis-bleed: decay outpacing time itself, freezing matter mid-collapse | still reads as "the freeze/slow one" |
| Lightning | **Discharge** | Overtaxed mechanical or temporal charge venting all at once, the same phenomenon behind the "massive cogs and the Thunderlord" seen in the sky under extreme instability | still reads as a shock/burst effect |
| Chaos | **Rust** | Raw, undiluted exposure to the Rust World itself, the source symptom rather than a byproduct | still reads as corruption/damage-over-time |
| Elemental / All | **Bleed** | General permeation across every symptom class at once, rather than one specific one | still reads as "the one that touches everything" |

Do not invent a seventh element. If a future system needs one, it must trace back
to a specific, nameable cause the way the six above do. "Because it's a cool
element" is not a cause.

## What Stays Out of This Myth

Rarity tiers, currency, affixes, and runewords/gems are **not** Rust-bleed
phenomena. Making every system about the Rust World flattens VRPG into "it's all
corruption," which contradicts the plain, mostly-mundane texture of the rest of
the game. See `history-and-factions.md` for rarity provenance, `economy.md` for
currency, and the style guide's naming-pattern section for affixes and
runewords/gems.

## World Texture Worth Knowing

Vintage Story does not generate discrete biomes. Regions form procedurally from
independent rock layers (igneous, metamorphic, sedimentary; 22 rock types total),
temperature, rainfall, and tree density. Ruins are scattered across the surface
and underground, sometimes containing blocks like aged wooden planks that cannot
be crafted, implying they predate the player's arrival. Nothing about the
element remap above needs to reference this directly, but it is useful texture
for item-flavor and location-flavor text: the world is geologically ordinary,
which is what makes Rust-bleed sites stand out.
