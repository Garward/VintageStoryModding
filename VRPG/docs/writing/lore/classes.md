# Classes

The six vanilla classes are the single best source of VRPG's target voice: every
line below is quoted directly from the base game, not paraphrased. When in doubt
about whether a piece of new flavor text sounds right, compare it against these
first.

## Commoner
> "Leave behind your old life. Crawl into the new world"

> "Commoners are generalists from a multitude of backgrounds. While they have no
> defining skills or strengths to speak of, they also lack the weaknesses of
> those who specialize."

## Hunter
> "Loose the arrow, kill the beast. Take it home to your dying child. Eventually
> your child will die and you'll find yourself providing for someone else's
> dying child."

> "Hunters are ranged specialists that excel in the wild forests, plains and
> mountains. They struggle in the confined tunnels of the underground."

## Malefactor
> "Terrible things are done in the heart of despair. She is gone, but you
> remain."

> "Malefactors are tenacious survivors who can avoid unwanted attention and find
> loot that others might have missed. A heavy conscience leads to weakness in
> combat, however."

## Clockmaker
> "The machines never stop ticking down there. You've seen to that. You can
> hear them in your sleep."

> "Clockmakers are masters of precision and systems. They can easily dismantle
> or repair machinery, but they have no stomach for violence."

## Blackguard
> "Fight if you must. Kill if you must. Don't look away. A hard gaze on life
> will see you through."

> "Blackguards are fierce soldiers that thrive in a close-ranged skirmish, but a
> life of conflict has its downsides. They have no talent for delicate work and
> need more food to maintain their strength."

## Tailor
> "Making a fitting garb, so we may walk with grace once more."

> "Tailors provide stylish clothes and clothing repairs to stay warm during the
> winter months. A lifetime spent in the city leaves them ill-prepared for some
> hardships of nature."

## What These Have In Common

Read as a set, not one at a time:

- Every quote is short. None run past two sentences before the period ends the
  thought.
- Every quote addresses the player directly or speaks in first person as the
  class, never in third-person marketing voice ("Experience the thrill of...").
- None of them use a hyphen or an em dash. Rhythm comes from short sentences and
  commas, not punctuation tricks.
- The tone is matter-of-fact even when the content is bleak ("She is gone, but
  you remain"). Nothing is played for shock or drama beyond what the plain
  statement already carries.
- Class trait names (Focused, Resourceful, Fleetfooted, Forager, Pilferer,
  Furtive, Precise, Technical, Tinkerer, Soldier, Hardy, Merciless, Ravenous,
  Nearsighted, Heavyhanded, Clothier, Mender, Civil, Weak, Kind) are single
  words or closed compounds, never hyphenated, never stacked into invented
  fantasy-name mashups. `Heavyhanded` in particular is the base game's own
  spelling of the exact word VRPG's `Heavy-Handed` affix should match; see the
  style guide's before/after table.
